# breakfast sandwich thyme for two

This is one of those recipes I never think to write down it's so ubiquitous. Fast food, the frozen aisle, even a local food truck turned brick-and-mortar fucks with this.

At home it's easy to throw together and invites a lot of experimentation. This version came about because there was a clamshell of thyme sprigs sitting out from dinner the night before and a sad little block of pepper jack sitting alone in the fridge. And of course with a little bit of one of the several chili crisps we keep on hand. The overall experience is an American standard breakfast sandwich: rich and buttery is the main riff; depth from the guajillo sitting in the pocket; and bright ornamentation from the black pepper, onion, and thyme.

But there's a lot of flexibility here, the foundation of sourdough and butter will carry just about anything you throw at it.

## cookware

- a toaster, preferably with asymmetric coils or bagel mode
- cast iron pan
- a fish turner or other thin, flexible spatula

## ingredients

Scales by the sandwich: one muffin, one egg, half the cheese and aromatics each.

- ~115g sourdough english muffins, two (store-bought unless you're making a day of it)
- ~100g large eggs, two
- ~20–30g unsalted butter, split between the pan, the aromatics, and the muffins (a guess, probably on the high side)
- ~28g pepper jack, sliced thin (maybe an ounce)
- ~10–15g green onion, one stalk, sliced fine
- a pinch of thyme leaves, two sprigs or so
- chili crisp oil (guajillo)
- hot sauce (a mild habanero/carrot is good here)
- black pepper
- salt

## steps

1. Heat the cast iron over low to medium-low. We'll be cooking the eggs over-easy in butter, which should melt but not quite brown and bubble.
2. While the cast iron is warming up, start double toasting the english muffins. The soft cragginess of the insides should become crackling and golden brown; toasting the insides toward the stronger toaster coils helps here.
3. Once the cast iron is hot, add butter, then crack the eggs in, using the turner to corral them into a round shape without breaking the yolks. Season the eggs here.
4. Let the eggs cook slowly on one side until the top whites and yolk begin to firm up. You can *turn* them but don't *flip* them. If you're getting an uneven cook (brown on the bottom, raw on top), use less heat, or cover to capture heat and steam to cook the tops.
5. As the eggs are cooking, add a little extra butter and pile up the green onions and thyme together to let them sweat it out a little in the pan.
6. Once the eggs are done it's time to assemble: butter the english muffins and add a little salt, lay cheese slices on one side and land the egg on that side, top the egg with chili oil, then add the green onion and thyme, and grind black pepper over the top.
7. Serve open-face so you can admire the plating and add hot sauce. Eat carefully closed-face over the plate to catch the yolk and chili oil that will immediately run down your hand and arm.
