# Berry Ice Cream Experiment — No-Egg Builds

**Started:** 2026-06-20 · **Last updated:** 2026-06-25
**Machine:** Cuisinart ICE-21P1 (1.5 qt). Booklet bases run 3 cups dairy; our build is a 2:1 half-and-half : cream. Bowl stored at 0 °F in the chest freezer.
**Status:** Dairy base ✅ dialed in (Father's Day hit). Vegan base — first churn 6/25, second to follow.

Both builds no-egg (berries up front, no egg-white orphans). One berry concentrate feeds everything.

## Berry concentrate (shared)

Drive the water out *before* it hits the base. Same logic as the old jam-flavored batches — but clear, seedless concentrate beats jam here: you get bright acidic raspberry or deep earthy huckleberry without seeds/skins in your teeth.

**Proven mixed batch:**
- 200 g raspberries + 200 g huckleberries (50/50)
- 100 g sugar (= 25% of fruit weight)
- 2 g salt

Cook down, smashing, reducing **fairly hard** but stopping short of candy territory — the target is *loose and pourable when cold*, not a set jam. Cool overnight to check.

**Fix the yield inconsistency → reduce to a target weight, not by eye.**
The mixed batch gave two half-pint jars; the straight 400 g batches each gave only one — partly the added sugar holding water, partly reducing the straights further. Stop guessing:
1. Tare the empty pot on the Triton.
2. Reduce until pot + contents hits a target number.
3. **Weigh the jar you were happy with and back-calculate that target**, so "the good one" becomes a number you can hit, not a vibe.
- Anchor: sugar at 25% of fruit weight; reduce to the mass that gave you the loose-when-cold pour.

**Pressing the pulp:** skip the mediocre strainer — use a **French press** (plunger + screen, lean on it). A potato ricer or a hard-wrung nut-milk bag also work.

**The leftover pulp → vinaigrette.** Immediate spot in the rotation. Anchor 3:1 oil:acid, whisk in pulp + salt + a little of the concentrate (or honey) + minced shallot/mustard to emulsify. (Fruit leather is the alternate fate — revisit if the cooking ever leans more granola-hiker.)

## Core 1 — Dairy, no-egg, pectin (FINALIZED)

Adapted from Alton's Serious Vanilla: Reloaded — vanilla swapped for berry, pectin dialed back because the berries bring their own acid + pectin. Sized to **retail units** (quart H&H + pint cream) so shopping is clean; the 6 cups of dairy split into **two 3-cup ICE-21 churns**.

**Master base (makes 2 churns):**
- 1 quart (4 cups) half-and-half
- 1 pint (2 cups) heavy cream
- 200 g sugar (into the dairy) + 100 g sugar (for the pectin step)
- ~3 g kosher salt
- 120 g water + 14 g liquid pectin (the pectin step)

**Per single churn (half the above):**
- 2 cups half-and-half + 1 cup heavy cream
- 100 g sugar (dairy) + 50 g sugar (pectin step)
- ~1.5 g salt
- 60 g water + 7 g liquid pectin
- ~100 g cooled concentrate (scaled from the proven 50g-in-1.5-cups; push to 120–130 g for louder fruit, knowing the extra fruit sugar softens it)

**Method:**
1. Heat half-and-half, cream, dairy sugar, salt to **185 °F** (~5 min). Kill heat, cover.
2. Boil water + pectin-step sugar + pectin **exactly 1 min**. Whisk into the dairy.
3. Ice-bath to room temp.
4. Fold in cooled concentrate. Taste warm — should read slightly *too* sweet (frozen mutes it).
5. Chill **< 40 °F** (≥ 4 h).
6. Churn (see machine notes). Soft-serve now, or freeze 3–4 h to harden.
7. Fold any mix-ins (crust, filling ribbons) in at the **container** stage, not the churn.

## Core 2 — Vegan, coconut + Oatly (STRETCH)

Sicilian-gelato logic: no egg, no dairy, body from a cooked-starch slurry. Coconut carries the fat (the hard-to-fake axis); Oatly softens the coconut and adds oat solids; guar's already in the can.

**Base (~1.5 cups liquid, one churn):**
- 1 cup full-fat coconut milk (shake the can first)
- ½ cup full-fat Oatly
- ¼ cup + 2 tbsp sugar (a touch more than dairy — less protein/fat to build structure)
- pinch salt
- **1 tbsp cornstarch**
- ~100 g cooled concentrate

**Method:**
1. Slurry cornstarch into 3 tbsp of the cold coconut/oat milk. Set aside.
2. Heat remaining coconut + Oatly + sugar + salt to a low simmer.
3. Whisk in slurry; cook 1–2 min until slightly thickened (starch gelatinizes). Kill heat.
4. Ice-bath to room temp; fold in concentrate.
5. Chill **< 40 °F**; churn.

**Alt stabilizer (pick ONE, for a clean read):** swap cornstarch for a pectin boil-step — 30 g water + 25 g sugar + ~3.5 g liquid pectin, boiled 1 min — letting the berries supply the acid, exactly like Core 1.

**Reading the first taste → pointing batch two (change exactly ONE):**
- Coconut reads as co-star → more Oatly / less coconut, or more concentrate to bury it.
- Thin or icy → missing fat (your milk ~16 g/100 ml vs cream's 20+). Push to coconut *cream*, or +½ tbsp cornstarch.
- Gummy, pudding-y cling → too much cornstarch; back off.
- Flat sweetness → vegan bases usually need a touch more sugar to read the same.

> Separate machine artifacts from recipe artifacts: a small cold load chunks on the walls *worse* than the dairy did. That's the machine, not the recipe — judge flavor and body, not wall chunks.

## Churn & machine notes

- **Cold bowl = fast + fine-grained.** The 0 °F chest-freezer bowl runs colder than a typical overnight-freezer bowl → ~17–18 min churn vs the usual 20–25, and colder freezing means smaller crystals. Good.
- **But fill the bowl.** A 1.5-cup load in a 1.5-qt bowl is half-empty — it freezes onto the walls before overrun develops, hence the hard sheets on the dasher. A full **3-cup** churn adds thermal mass and mostly fixes the wall-chunking. (This is why each dairy churn is sized to 3 cups.)
- **Seizing on the dasher** near the end is normal; prod to keep it moving, but past a point there's no more mixing to be had — that's your stop.
- **Hardened hold:** lean ~18% Philadelphia base softens fast in heat. Only chase it by nudging the cream up if you want cookout hold.
- **Vegan caveat:** expect *more* wall-chunking on the smaller, leaner vegan load. Machine artifact — don't read it as failure.

---

## Levers if something's off

- **Icy / grainy** → too much free water. Reduce concentrate harder, or bump stabilizer.
- **Gummy / short / pasty** (dairy) → pectin over-set by berry acid. Cut pectin further.
- **Too soft, fast-melting** → berry monosaccharides depress the freeze point. Pull back a little base sugar, or shift some to corn syrup for crystal control without added sweetness.
- **Coconut too loud** (vegan) → more Oatly, less coconut milk.
