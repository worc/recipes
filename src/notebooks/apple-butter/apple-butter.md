# Apple Butter: Batch 01

Unspiced, general-purpose apple butter from the two backyard trees. Lab notebook; newest results first.

## Goal

A plain apple butter base for later use: packed or canned now, spiced downstream. Candidate uses: pie filling starter, meat glaze, dessert topper, apple spice syrup base (for this year's lattes).

## Inputs (as used)

| Item | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Apples, backyard (variety unknown) | 1 lb, post-trim | Peeled, quartered, cored, heavy wormhole trim ("rustic") |
| White sugar | ¼ cup (~50 g) | Light brown sugar and blackstrap held back for a later variant |
| Water | ¼ cup | Cider vinegar held back for a later variant |
| Peel stock | ~¼ cup (one ladle) | Peels covered with water, boiled, simmered. Glossy and cloudy; pectin content unknown |

**Equipment:** 6-qt enameled Dutch oven, second pot for peel stock, potato masher. Stick blender skipped; one pound doesn't give it enough depth to work in.

**Oven:** set to 285°F, aiming for ~275°F actual. Oven runs cool; direction known, magnitude (~10°F) assumed.

## Process

1. Apples, sugar, and water into the Dutch oven. Bring to a boil, then down to a simmer.
2. In parallel: peels into a second pot, covered with water, boiled, then down to a simmer.
3. T+15 min: mash apples. Add one ladle (~¼ cup) of peel stock, mash again.
4. Taste check.
5. Into the oven, lid on but ajar. Stir and scrape periodically; small batch needs more attention than full-size recipes assume.
6. Pull when doneness criteria are met.

## Doneness criteria

- A spoon dragged across the bottom leaves a channel that stays open for a few seconds.
- Cold plate: a dollop holds its mound with no watery halo after a minute.
- Deep, even brown; glossy surface.

## Observation log (newest first)

### After the bake: peel stock pectin test

- Leftover peel stock, which stayed in its pot through the ~2 hr bake, dropped into 99% isopropyl: formed a clear gel clump ("apple snot"). High pectin.
- Caveat: this stock had ~2 more hours of extraction and evaporation than the ¼ cup added before baking. The earlier addition was likely weaker; strength at that point is unknown.
- Confirms the instinct, and the old method of using apple peels as a pectin source (huckleberry canning).

### Pulled at T+2:00 in oven: results

- **Color:** rich, like brown butter or a dark roux. Lighter than a gumbo roux, a BBQ sauce, or store-bought apple butter.
- **Flavor:** good overall. Rich, apple-forward, sweet. White sugar alone got to a dark-rum depth of sweetness.
- **Faults:** bits of peel from rough processing, and a faint bitterness. Expected to improve with the food mill and clean pulp separation.
- **Spice:** warming spices would suit it, but the unspiced base stands on its own.
- **Yield:** about ½ pint (two ¼-pint quilted jelly jars). Above the ½–¾ cup estimate.
- **Disposition:** refrigerate. Freeze if not used within a couple of days. Fine to toss, since the production batch supersedes it.

### In oven, ~11:00: pH check

- Brewing-supply test strip: reads ~5, possibly 4. Strip resolution is coarse, and the brown sauce may skew the color read.
- Decision: freeze this batch rather than water-bath can it.

### Into the oven

- Lid on, ajar.
- Shallow layer in a 6-qt pot: expect faster reduction and more edge scorch risk than full-size recipes.

### Stovetop, T+15: mashed, peel stock added

- Taste: bright, tart, sweet applesauce. A big yes.
- Browning quickly, with no acid added.
- Peel stock glossy and cloudy.

## Yield

- Final weight / volume: about ½ pint (two ¼-pint quilted jelly jars); see the results entry above.
- Estimate before finishing: roughly ½ to ¾ cup. Apple butter usually reduces to about half the volume of the sauce.

## Conclusions

- The simplest method works: apples, white sugar, water, peel stock, about 2 hours in the oven.
- Approach: process the apples now into a plain base, then canned or frozen. Spice and specialize at the point of use.

## Open questions / next time

- **Bitterness source.** Peel bits are the main suspect. Rule out waterline scorch in the production batch by scraping the rim more often.
- **Small-batch variant: cider vinegar as the starting liquid.** Replace part of the water. Expect brighter flavor, more canning margin, and possibly lighter color, since lower pH slows Maillard browning.
- **Weight basis.** Batch 01 was weighed post-trim. Next time, weigh before trim too, to get a trim-loss figure for these trees.
- **Acidity.** Strip reading of ~4–5 means production batches get acidified (citric or the citric/malic blend) and verified with a pH meter, targeting ≤4.0, before any water-bath canning.
- **Production scale.** Planned as a doubled 5 lb recipe in the SSLB-15 brazier (largest vessel that fits the oven), repeated two or three times. A 2.3 qt food mill this year, upgrading only if throughput demands it.
- **Peel stock pectin.** Alcohol test passed after a long simmer (see log). Next time, test at the moment of addition to know what actually went in.
- **Food mill byproduct.** Simmer the skins and cores the mill rejects into pectin stock. Uses: loosening the butter, or as a natural pectin for low-pectin fruit like huckleberries.
- **Variant axes held back today:** light brown sugar or blackstrap for some of the white sugar; cider vinegar for some of the water.
- **Cider from apple butter?** Dilute, add pectic enzyme for the haze, and ferment. Open question.
