# yogurt chicken with figs

Yogurt-marinated chicken thighs, seared then finished with a fig, pomegranate molasses, and toasted pepper pan sauce. Over rice.

Loosely after [The Briny Lemon's yogurt-marinated chicken with apricot-fig sauce](https://thebrinylemon.blogspot.com/2016/03/yogurt-marinated-chicken-with-apricot_22.html), but swapped to thighs, pantry dried peppers instead of paprika, preserved lemon instead of fresh, and pomegranate molasses in place of the preserves and vinegar.

This is v1 as cooked. See **next time** at the bottom before cooking it again — the big change is that this wants to be a braise from the start.

## cookware
- spice grinder or mortar and pestle
- bowl + zip bag for the marinade
- v1 used the 8" cast iron (size 7) plus an oven finish — **too small, see next time**

## ingredients

### pepper powder
- 2g dried fermented calabrian chiles
- 12g dried aleppo
- toast together, then grind. makes ~14g, about half goes in the marinade, the rest in the sauce

### marinade
- 600g chicken thighs, boneless skinless
- 110g greek yogurt (looks wet in the bag, still a thinner coat than the source recipe's ratio)
- 6g garlic (3 cloves), grated
- 4–6g preserved lemon rind (~¼ lemon), minced
- 7g pepper powder (enough to turn the yogurt red, not enough to teargas the chicken)
- 3g salt (v1; ~0.5%, deliberately light — **go to ~6g / 1% next time**)

### sauce
- 21g dried figs, chopped (ours were very dry and a bit green)
- 10g pomegranate molasses
- 10g hot water (~130°F) to start the steep, +10g boiling water when that wasn't enough, more during the finish
- light oil for the sear and the shallots (probably the usual peanut oil)
- 60g shallot (one), diced
- the remaining pepper powder (~7g)
- the remaining salt, to bring the whole dish to about 1%

### to serve
- rice
- lettuce shreds, dressed with lemon and za'atar
- a spoonful of greek yogurt on top

## steps

1. Toast and grind the pepper powder.
2. Mix the marinade in a bowl **first**, then decant the chicken and marinade into the bag. Mixing around chicken that's already in the bag is tedious.
3. Marinate. v1 went 3 hours, which was plenty for flavor; thighs don't need the 8+ hours breasts do.
4. Start the figs steeping in the pomegranate molasses and hot water.
5. Wipe the marinade off the thighs and pat dry. What's left on the surface is water you have to cook off before anything browns.
6. Sear over medium-high, not max. The yogurt milk sugars and the aleppo brown fast. In v1 the fond got gumbo-roux dark and I pulled the thighs early to save it.
7. In the same pan: sweat the shallots with the rest of the pepper powder, add the rest of the salt, then the figs and their liquid, plus more water to get the figs to soften.
8. Thighs back in, into the oven at 350°F to bring the sauce together and get the thighs up to temp. v1 only did 5–10 minutes here (hungry) and the texture suffered.
9. Plate over rice with the dressed lettuce alongside, sauce over the chicken, a spoonful of yogurt on top. Final salt at the plate — good excuse for the seasoned salts.

## v1 notes
- Sear and exterior were good. 3-hour marinade was enough.
- Texture was so-so: improving in the oven, but pulled too early. 5–10 min doesn't convert thigh collagen; safe at 165°F but thighs get good closer to 175–195°F.
- The plating yogurt is what pulls everything together. Cool dairy fat against the smoky/sweet heat.
- Pomegranate molasses covered both the sweet and the acid the source recipe got from preserves + vinegar.
- Very dry figs needed a lot more water and time than the steep gave them.

## next time (v2)
- **Pan:** the 6-qt enameled dutch oven (sear everything in one batch, lid, straight into the oven), or the All-Clad D3 12" with lid. All the chicken in at once covers more of the pan floor and buys buffer before the fond scorches.
- **Salt the marinade at the normal ratio:** ~1% (≈6g on 600g), even though it *looks* like a lot.
- **Plan the braise from the start:** the sear only needs to color the outside. Then figs, liquid, thighs back in, ~25–40 min at 325–350°F, partly covered. Done by fork-tenderness, not the clock (count up, record what it actually took).
- **Let the figs reconstitute in the braise** instead of fighting them with steep-and-top-up. Start with more water.
